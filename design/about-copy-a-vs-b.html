A · The part you're hiring direct · belief first

ABOUT

Software can assemble a video now. It can't tell you which take holds the scene. That part is a person. That's the part I do.

I'm Artem Pichak. I've been editing, grading, and shooting for over a decade. Right now that means vertical drama series for TikTok and Instagram, YouTube vlogs and tech reviews, commercials, documentaries for Ukrainian television, music videos, automotive films. The formats keep changing. The job doesn't.

I use AI where it earns its place. Transcripts, sorting rushes, a rough first pass. It's good at the tedious work, and I'm glad to hand that over. But it doesn't know when a moment needs to sit a second longer, or what the story underneath the footage actually is. Finding that is the work. Whether the piece runs 40 seconds or 20 minutes, my job is the same: make the story land.

I've done that for Panasonic, Sony, Sennheiser, Renault, Mojang, and for small teams nobody's heard of yet. The size of the client changes the schedule. It doesn't change how I cut.

B · Ten years, every format warmer · belief through experience

ABOUT

I've spent more than a decade cutting other people's footage into stories. The tools keep changing. The reason people call me hasn't.

These days I'm cutting vertical drama, the short scripted series made for TikTok and Instagram. Before that it was YouTube shows, tech reviews, brand films, documentaries for Ukrainian TV, music videos. I've worked for Samsung and Xiaomi and Caterpillar, and for a two-person startup shooting on a borrowed camera. I like that range. Every format teaches you something the others can't.

Here's what a decade in the edit bay taught me. The hard part was never the software. It's knowing which of nine takes is alive, when to hold a shot one beat past comfortable, and what the piece is really about, which is rarely what the brief says. AI helps me get to that part faster now. It handles transcripts, sorts the rushes, drafts an assembly. I'm happy to let it. The decisions it hands me are still mine to make.

If you need a video that just exists, there are cheaper ways to get one. If you need it to land, that's me.
