ABOUT

portrait

Almost everything about making video can be automated now. The part that keeps someone watching - can't.

I'm Artem Pichak. I've been editing, grading, and shooting for more than a decade. Right now that means vertical drama series for TikTok and Instagram, YouTube vlogs and tech reviews, commercials, documentaries for Ukrainian television, music videos, automotive films. The formats keep changing. The job doesn't.

The hard part was never the software. It's knowing which of nine takes is alive, when to hold a shot one beat past comfortable, and what the piece is really about, which is rarely what the brief says.

If you need a video that just exists, there are cheaper ways to get one. If you need it to land, that's me.

Grading in DaVinci Resolve. Matching mixed cameras, keeping skin honest, holding one look shot to shot.

Teaching

Since 2017 I've taught editing at the ProCut school, in municipal programs, and one to one. Some of them edit as a hobby. Some needed it for the job they already had. Some cut for major TV channels and production companies now. Explaining a cut is the best way I know to understand it.

On naming creators, and on the closing line

The idea underneath is right - the people with the most automation available still hire humans. Naming them is what I'd avoid. It asserts how someone else works, which we can't verify and they can change (MrBeast has publicly experimented with AI dubbing and thumbnail testing), and a wrong claim about a famous creator is the kind that gets corrected in public.

"The biggest channels on the internet" makes the same point, can't be falsified, and doesn't borrow anyone's name. If you do want names, the safe form is quoting something they've actually said on the record, with a link.

Separately: the closing line ("there are cheaper ways to get one") is Alex's pick over my objection that it answers an objection nobody has made yet. Recording that here so it reads as a decision rather than an oversight. A softer form, if it ever grates: "Plenty of people can make you a video. I'd rather make you one that lands."

Where this is still guessing

The whole section now rests on a belief I wrote for Artem, which is the same failure as the copy it replaces - written for him rather than by him. Ten minutes of him talking about why he thinks the human part matters would replace all three drafts with something none of us would think to write.

One smaller one: "which of nine takes is alive" is the most editor-sounding line here and it is still invented. Ask him for the real version - a moment where the take that looked right on paper was wrong on screen.

The teaching range is now his, from Alex: students run from hobbyists through people who needed editing for the job they already had, to people cutting for major TV channels and production companies. Still worth confirming whether the 2,000 figure covers the school, the municipal programs and the private lessons together, since the number sits directly above a sentence naming all three.
